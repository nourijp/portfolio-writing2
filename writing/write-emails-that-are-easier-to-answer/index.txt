15:T4b7,<p>Before sending an email, check whether the reader can identify the request and act on it.</p>
<h2>Put the request near the beginning</h2>
<p>Give enough context to explain the situation, then state what you need.</p>
<p>For example, “Please confirm which delivery date we should use in the customer notice. The current schedule lists both Tuesday and Thursday.”</p>
<h2>Make several questions easy to scan</h2>
<p>If you need more than one decision, use a short list.</p>
<ul>
<li>Which delivery date is confirmed?</li>
<li>Should the notice include an alternative contact?</li>
<li>Who should review the message before it is sent?</li>
</ul>
<p>Separate questions help the reader respond to each point.</p>
<h2>Include the timing</h2>
<p>Explain when you need the information and what depends on it. A deadline is more useful when the reader understands the next step.</p>
<h2>Review the recipient list</h2>
<p>Include the people who need to act or understand the decision. Check whether the message gives each of them enough context.</p>
<p>Read the email once from the recipient&#39;s perspective. If the likely response is “What do you need me to do?”, revise the request before sending.</p>

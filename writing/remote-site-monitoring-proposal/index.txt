15:T134d,<h2>Illustrative Client Brief</h2>
<p>A property manager seeks a 30-day pilot across three commercial properties, covering selected exterior areas from 7 p.m. to 7 a.m. Central Time daily. The request calls for video event review, an approved escalation process, a record of reviewed events, and a final report to support an expansion decision.</p>
<h2>Executive Summary</h2>
<p>We propose one coordinated monitoring service for all three properties, with a shared event log and a named service coordinator. Operators would review video alerts during the coverage window, assess them against approved event categories, and notify the designated contacts when action is required.</p>
<p>This approach gives your managers a consistent record of what occurred, who was contacted, and which issues remain open. A 30-day pilot would let you evaluate service delivery and reporting usefulness before committing to broader coverage.</p>
<h2>Requirements and Response</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Client requirement</th>
<th>Proposed delivery</th>
<th>Verification</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td>R1  After-hours coverage</td>
<td>Review alerts from approved cameras at all three sites during the stated hours.</td>
<td>Activation test at each site and a record of coverage interruptions.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>R2  Defined escalation</td>
<td>Use approved event categories and a primary and backup contact for each site.</td>
<td>Test notifications and retain the approved contact plan.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>R3  Traceable events</td>
<td>Record each reviewed alert, disposition, notification, and follow-up owner.</td>
<td>Reconcile reviewed alert IDs with event-log entries.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>R4  Expansion decision</td>
<td>Deliver a consolidated pilot report within three business days of completion.</td>
<td>Client review of results against the four requirements.</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<h2>Service Scope</h2>
<p>The pilot covers event-triggered video review, client notifications, event records, and reporting. The client retains responsibility for on-site response. Operators would follow a written escalation plan identifying who to contact, when to use a backup, and how to document the outcome. Camera additions, equipment replacement, and on-site services would require a separate scope.</p>
<h2>Implementation and Responsibilities</h2>
<ol>
<li><p><strong>Confirm site readiness.</strong> The service coordinator and client property manager identify camera views, coverage gaps, connectivity needs, authorized access, and privacy requirements. The client provides site information and confirms primary and backup contacts.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Approve and test the plan.</strong> Operations prepares the coverage and escalation plans. The client approves both. Technical staff test alert delivery, video access, and notifications at every site. Activation follows successful testing and approval of the commercial terms.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Operate the pilot.</strong> Operators review alerts and document dispositions during the coverage window. The coordinator reports service interruptions and unresolved handoffs to the property manager and holds a weekly review of open issues.</p>
</li>
<li><p><strong>Evaluate the results.</strong> Within three business days after the 30-day pilot, the coordinator delivers the final report and meets with the client to recommend expansion, adjustment, or further evaluation.</p>
</li>
</ol>
<h2>Reporting and Pilot Evaluation</h2>
<p>The final report would show reviewed alerts by site, category, and time period, with timestamps for review and notification. It would identify recurring activity, contact failures, coverage interruptions, and follow-up items, giving managers a basis for adjusting contacts or coverage.</p>
<p>Evaluation would check delivery against R1 through R4, including event-log completeness and successful test notifications. Any gaps in the evidence would be identified. The property manager would also assess whether the report provides enough context to assign follow-up and decide where additional coverage is justified.</p>
<h2>Commercial Basis</h2>
<p>The commercial schedule would price the three-site pilot against the specified hours, approved camera count, and reporting scope. It would separate recurring monitoring fees from any setup or equipment charges.</p>
<p>Sales and operations would confirm that the scope can be delivered. Finance would validate pricing, and legal would review contractual terms before submission of a binding offer. The client would approve any additional equipment, scope changes, and associated charges in writing.</p>
<h2>Requested Client Decision</h2>
<p>Confirm the three pilot properties and nominate a property manager for a site-readiness meeting. That meeting would resolve equipment and access dependencies, allowing us to present a priced service schedule and proposed activation date for approval.</p>
